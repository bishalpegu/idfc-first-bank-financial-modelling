# IDFC FIRST Bank — Financial Modelling & Valuation

### Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) and Comparable Company Analysis

**Company:** IDFC FIRST Bank Ltd.  
**Sector:** Banking | Financial Services  
**Project Type:** Financial Modelling | Equity Valuation  
**Tool:** Microsoft Excel

---

## 1. Project Overview

This project presents a financial modelling and valuation analysis of IDFC FIRST Bank Ltd.

The Excel workbook organises historical financial information, model assumptions, financial calculations and valuation outputs to support an assessment of the bank's financial performance and implied equity value.

The analysis incorporates Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) valuation and Comparable Company Analysis to examine valuation under different methodologies.

## 2. Project Objectives

- Organise and analyse historical financial information.
- Develop a structured financial modelling framework.
- Document assumptions used in the model.
- Estimate valuation using the DCF methodology implemented in the workbook.
- Compare the bank's valuation with relevant publicly traded peers.
- Consolidate key model outputs for interpretation and review.

## 3. Model Structure

The workbook contains seven worksheets.

## 4. Valuation Methodologies

### A. Discounted Cash Flow Analysis

The DCF section applies the methodology implemented in the workbook to estimate equity value based on projected financial performance and the relevant valuation assumptions.

The interpretation of the results depends on the cash-flow definition, discount rate, terminal value assumptions and treatment of capital and other balance-sheet items.

### B. Comparable Company Analysis

The comparable-company section evaluates the bank against selected publicly traded peers using the valuation multiples included in the workbook.

Peer selection, accounting comparability, business mix and differences in growth, profitability and asset quality are important considerations when interpreting relative valuations.

## 5. Key Analytical Considerations

The analysis focuses on the relationship between historical financial performance, modelling assumptions and valuation outcomes.

Important considerations include:

- Consistency and reliability of historical financial data.
- Transparency of forecast assumptions.
- Sensitivity of valuation to key inputs.
- Comparability of selected banking peers.
- Differences between intrinsic and relative valuation estimates.
